02.1

What makes Robot Motion Hesitant? Producing and Perceiving Hesitancy in Robotic Manipulators

ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) · 2027 · Under review

Eleven trained dancers kinesthetically taught a manipulator 66 free-form reaching trajectories at three commanded levels of hesitancy: slight, significant, and extreme. Characterizing them across geometry, timing, smoothness, and amplitude shows that higher hesitancy is produced as a longer, more circuitous reach with more retreats. A model decodes the commanded level at κ = 0.76 under leave-dancers-out validation, and an in-person study with 33 unprimed observers finds that the degree of hesitancy is carried chiefly by elapsed time, because the dancers travel further rather than slowing down.

02.2

Dance2Hesitate: A Multi-Modal Dataset of Dancer-Taught Hesitancy for Understandable Robot Motion

Designing Transparent and Understandable Robots (D-TUR) Workshop at HRI · 2026

An open-source, multi-modal dataset of dancer-generated hesitant motion: 66 kinesthetic-teaching demonstrations on a Franka Emika Panda reaching toward a Jenga tower at three graded hesitancy levels, plus synchronized RGB-D motion capture of 84 upper-limb and 70 whole-body dancer trajectories.

02.3

Employing Laban Shape for Generating Emotionally and Functionally Expressive Trajectories in Robotic Manipulators

IEEE International Conference on Robot and Human Interactive Communication (RO-MAN) · 2025

Uses Laban notation to give manipulators functional expressivity: two new variants of hesitant motion, spoke-like and arc-like, that participants associate with reduced robot competency, and Happy, Sad, Shy, and Angry trajectories enhanced with Laban Shape that read as distinct emotions on the valence-arousal-dominance spectrum.

02.4

BBoE: Leveraging a Bundle of Edges for Kinodynamic Bidirectional Motion Planning

IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L) · 2026 · Under review

A bidirectional, kinodynamic, sampling-based planner that reuses a precomputed bundle of robot motions to converge quickly in cluttered environments. Across four systems spanning 3D to 6D state spaces, BBoE is the only planner to reach ≥96% success on every system, with the lowest planning time on three of the four.
