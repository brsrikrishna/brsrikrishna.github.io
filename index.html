About

I am a fourth-year PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, advised by Alessandro Roncone in the HIRO Group. My research develops learning-based motion generation models and sampling-based kinodynamic planners, deployed on real manipulator hardware rather than stopping at simulation. Lately that has meant a diffusion model that generates Franka Emika Panda trajectories at a commanded level of hesitancy, and BBoE, a bidirectional kinodynamic planner for cluttered environments.

Before Boulder I earned a B.Tech in Electrical and Electronics Engineering from PES University in Bengaluru, then spent a year as a research assistant in the Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Lab at the Indian Institute of Science, working on heterogeneous multi-robot task allocation with TurtleBot3 and ROS 2. Research

02.1

Human–Robot Interaction

I study hesitancy in robot motion: how a manipulator can move so a person can tell it is unsure, and how that motion is perceived. This spans the dancer-taught Dance2Hesitate dataset, an in-person perception study with 33 participants, and expressive trajectories generated with Laban Movement Analysis.

02.2

Motion Planning

I build sampling-based kinodynamic planners for systems where dynamics, not just geometry, decide what is reachable. BBoE searches bidirectionally over a bundle of precomputed edges, producing solutions 1.5× faster than state-of-the-art planners in cluttered environments.

02.3

Generative Models for Robotics

I train a hesitancy-conditioned diffusion model in PyTorch that generates real Franka Emika Panda trajectories at a commanded level of hesitancy. Transformer and LSTM decoders handle the inverse problem, classifying hesitancy intensity from motion at 0.76 leave-one-dancer-out on unseen performers.
